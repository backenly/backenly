## Why this is separate from the license

Section 6 of the Apache License 2.0 says so directly:

> This License does not grant permission to use the trade names, trademarks,
> service marks, or product names of the Licensor, except as required for
> reasonable and customary use in describing the origin of the Work and
> reproducing the content of the NOTICE file.

The copyright licence and the trademark are two different things. Apache-2.0
gives you broad rights over the code and deliberately gives you none over the
name. This document explains how we apply that.

The reason is not control over the software. It is that users need one reliable
answer to the question "is this actually Backenly?" A name that could mean
anyone's build is worth nothing to the people relying on it.

## If you fork this project

You are welcome to fork it. Apache-2.0 gives you that right and we are not going
to argue with it.

When you distribute your fork, you must:

1. **Give it a different name.** Not Backenly. Not a name a reasonable person
   would confuse with Backenly.
2. **Use your own logo and visual identity.**
3. **Keep the attribution.** Apache-2.0 §4 requires you to retain the `LICENSE`,
   the `NOTICE` file, all copyright and trademark notices, and to mark the files
   you changed. This is a condition of the licence, not a courtesy.
4. **Be accurate about the relationship.** "Derived from Backenly" is correct
   and welcome. "Backenly" or "Backenly Pro" or "Backenly Community Edition" is
   not.

Stripping the `LICENSE` or `NOTICE` file does not create a new project. It
places the distribution outside the Apache-2.0 grant entirely, which makes it
straightforward copyright infringement rather than a licensing dispute.

Authorship is a matter of public record. The commit history, the release tags,
